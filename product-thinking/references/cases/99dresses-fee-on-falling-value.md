**Decision.** 99dresses was a mobile marketplace for trading clothes. It charged a fee based on the value of each traded item. Late on, it made one more pivot that its research supported.

**Outcome.** Within 3 months of its US launch it did over 1,000 trades a week. Stock turnover rose from 17% to 50%, and retention was strong. Then growth slowed, and the average value of listed items fell. Trade volume grew, but revenue did not. After the last pivot, top-line metrics jumped, but transactions, the one metric that counted, fell sharply. The bridge round failed, and the company shut down in 2014.

**Lesson.** If revenue depends on a value that can drift down, volume growth can hide a business that does not work.
