**Decision.** Mark Hendrickson launched Plancast in November 2009. Users posted their upcoming plans so friends could see them and join. He raised a seed round in early 2010.

**Outcome.** Plancast passed 100,000 registrations and about 230,000 monthly visitors, but it did not grow further. In a January 2012 post-mortem, Hendrickson gave the reasons. People have few plans to share: he had 5 upcoming plans listed against almost 2,800 tweets over the same time. Most people avoid early commitments. Sharing a plan gave little reward compared to sharing a photo. Many people prefer private invitations to public plans. Plans lose value once the event is over, and they only matter to people nearby.

**Lesson.** Social event planning has failed for structural reasons, so a new attempt must explain which of those reasons it removes.
