**Decision.** Notion's first beta was a programming tool for people who could not code. It was built on a weak tech stack and crashed often. Users did not want it. Ivan Zhao said they focused on what they wanted to give the world, not on what the world wanted from them. In 2015 the angel money ran low. The founders fired their 4-person team, sublet the San Francisco office, and moved to Kyoto to cut costs. There they rebuilt the product from scratch.

**Outcome.** Notion 1.0 launched in August 2016, and tech early adopters took it up fast. Notion 2.0 shipped in March 2018 with fewer than 10 employees. Notion reached 1M users in September 2019 on seed funding alone. It raised at a $2B valuation in January 2020 and at $10B in October 2021, with 20M users.

**Lesson.** When users reject the product, cut burn to buy time and rebuild around what users want, not around what you wanted to build.
