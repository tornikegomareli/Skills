**Decision.** After the Apple Watch announcement, Pebble moved away from its hacker early adopters. It positioned Pebble Time first as a productivity watch, then as a fitness watch. In 2015 it also doubled operating expenses in expectation of growth, and it ordered holiday stock before retail results came in.

**Outcome.** Revenue grew from about $30M (2013) to $60M (2014), then only 35% in 2015. Pebble targeted about $100M in 2015 sales and reached $82M. Gross margin fell from 35% to 27%, and over $15M of inventory sat in warehouses at the end of 2015. The founder says the team did no product research and did not talk to enough users. Pebble sold parts of the business to Fitbit at the end of 2016.

**Lesson.** Leaving a segment that loves the product for a broader market you have not researched, while costs double, can end a company that already found fit.
