**Decision.** Sonar was a social app that alerted you when friends were near. The team chose engagement as its main metric. It built a LinkedIn integration because people asked for it. It spent 6 weeks on a side project with Wired magazine, and it rushed copycat features when a competitor got press. Shares from the app carried a plain text string that users disliked.

**Outcome.** Engagement rose by orders of magnitude, but investors cared about growth. The LinkedIn integration had no effect, because the people who asked were not real users. Users stopped sharing the bland updates, and Facebook began to hide Sonar posts. The Wired project gave no lasting benefit. Sonar raised nearly $2M and had millions of downloads, then stalled when its category cooled in 2012.

**Lesson.** For a social product, pick growth as the early metric and protect the sharing path that brings new users.
