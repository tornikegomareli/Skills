**Decision.** Google released Glass Explorer Edition in 2013 for $1,500. It was a head-mounted display with a camera, controlled by voice and a touchpad. Google meant it as a prototype to learn from, but let the public treat it as a finished consumer product. For consumers it stayed more novelty than necessity.

**Outcome.** Privacy worries and the "Glasshole" label followed, and some venues banned it. Google ended the Explorer program in January 2015. In March 2015 Astro Teller of Google X said the mistake was encouraging too much attention. The team said Explorer had shown the most potential at work, where Glass kept hands free, so it moved to that use. In July 2017 Google reported Glass Enterprise Edition at more than 50 companies. AGCO cut production time by 25% and inspection time by 30%. GE Aviation mechanics gained 8 to 12% in efficiency. Sutter Health doctors saved about two hours a day on records. Google stopped selling Glass in March 2023.

**Lesson.** The consumer device showed what the hardware could do, while the enterprise version showed what a worker could now do, and only the second earned users.
