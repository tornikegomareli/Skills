**Decision.** On 31 July 2013, two weeks before the preview release, CEO Stewart Butterfield sent the team a memo. It said Slack sells a change for the team, not chat software. The outcomes he named were less information overload, a searchable record of team talk, faster decisions, and less email. The memo used a saddle shop as the image: sell horseback riding, not saddles. It also asked for high polish. New users arrive with no context and little patience, so small irritations must go.

**Outcome.** Slack came from the internal chat tool of Glitch, a game that stopped in late 2012. After the August 2013 public launch, 8,000 customers signed up in 24 hours. Slack passed 1M daily active users in late 2015 and more than 10M by April 2019. Salesforce announced a purchase for about $27.7B in December 2020 and closed it in July 2021.

**Lesson.** Describe the product by the change it makes for the customer, and polish the first contact for people who do not yet know why they need it.
