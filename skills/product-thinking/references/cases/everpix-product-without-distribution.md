**Decision.** Everpix spent about 18 months perfecting a photo organizer before its 1.0 release in March 2013. It spent almost nothing on marketing. The team rejected a sharing flow that would have made friends sign up, because they found it needy. It did not position itself against free tools from Apple and Google.

**Outcome.** By March 2013 it had fewer than 19,000 sign-ups, and it had spent most of its $1.8M on the product. Users loved it: 12.4% of free users paid, and about half returned each week. At the end it had about 50,000 users, 7,000 subscribers, and $40,000 a month in sales, which did not cover fixed costs. VCs doubted a large subscription business in a market of free photo tools. The Series A failed, and Everpix announced its shutdown on 5 November 2013.

**Lesson.** A loved product still dies if nobody plans how new users will find it and why they should pick it over the free default.
