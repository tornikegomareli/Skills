**Claim.** If a product does not say what customers use today instead of it, it cannot say why it is better, and users do not know when to choose it.

## Why it is a problem
Dunford defines positioning as five linked parts: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target customers, and market category. She starts with the alternatives, meaning what a customer would do if the product did not exist. Unique attributes and value only make sense in comparison with that alternative. The alternative is often a spreadsheet, a manual process, a hire, or doing nothing, and not a direct competitor. She also warns against "phantom competitors" that never appear in real deals. A README or landing page that lists features with no comparison leaves the positioning work to the reader.

## How to detect
- Read the README first screen, landing page copy, and app store text. Look for a named alternative ("instead of X", "unlike X", "without X").
- Check whether the target customer is narrow (role, situation) or broad ("everyone", "teams").
- Check whether each listed feature says what it lets the user do that the alternative does not.
- Check whether the product claims a market category. If it claims none, readers will guess one.
- If the docs are silent, ask intake questions 4 and 5 (what they use today, what made them look for something better).

## Fixes
- List the top 2 or 3 real alternatives, including "do nothing" and "spreadsheet". Rewrite the first paragraph to compare against them.
- Name one narrow target customer for whom the difference is largest.
- Map each key feature to the value it gives over the alternative. Drop features from the pitch that do not map.

## Cases
- `everpix-product-without-distribution` (failure)
- `ondsel-hobbyists-not-buyers` (failure)
- `rethinkdb-wrong-quality-metrics` (failure)
- `tract-ai-planning-editor` (failure)
